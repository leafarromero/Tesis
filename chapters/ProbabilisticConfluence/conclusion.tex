\section{Conclusion}
In this chapter we have analyzed the different possibilities to
transform a simple probabilistic calculus into a probabilistically
/computationally confluent calculus. The main result is Theorem 
\ref{thm:confmodulo}, which proves that we can relax the affinity
restriction on ``non-interfering'' paths. This technique has been
considered in the author master's thesis~\cite{Romero20} to
prove the computational confluence of the quantum lambda calculus
$\lambda_\rho$~\cite{lambdarho}.

\paragraph{Deferred and implicit measurement principles.}
In the context of quantum of computation, there are two results
which grant some freedom over the precise moment when to measure
a qubit. The \emph{deferred measurement principle} states that
intermediate measurements can be moved to the end of a circuit;
if the results are used to classically control gates, these can
be replaced by quantum-controlled operations. The \emph{implicit
measurement principle} specifies that, without loss of generality,
any non-measured qubit at the end of the circuit may be assumed to
be measured.

With these two principles in mind, for the following two chapters,
we will focus on deterministic quantum lambda calculi which do not
perform measurements. This is a deliberate choice, since the inclusion of
measurement operations also adds the need to account for probabilistic
evolutions. We will study different characteristics of these calculi,
but model them with insights from this chapter to make a future
incorporation of measurement as seamlessly as possible.
